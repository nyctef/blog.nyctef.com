tl;dr I enjoyed the movie, spoilers below.

- The Force Awakens was a lot of fun to watch, although I was a lot more excited about what could come next rather than what happened in this film. I think I’ll probably like it a lot more when the trilogy is complete.

- Damn, everyone’s terrible with lightsabers in this continuity. Finn practically picks up a lightsaber and immediately goes toe-to-toe with Ren, a trained Force-sensitive duelist, and holds his own for an impressive amount of time. In almost any other Star Wars setting he’d be dead in seconds. A decided lack of backflips in this one.

- I bet Luke is just holding off on the lightsaber training so he can kick everyone else’s ass if needs be.

- Talking about Luke: the fact that he’s gone off to have a mope after training Kylo didn’t work out is a _very_ Jedi thing to do. I love it. Making the Jedi fallible makes Star Wars so much more interesting, rather than a really obvious True Good vs Total Evil excuse for lightsaber duels.

- I want to see what corrupted Kylo in the first place. He obviously has some issues he has to deal with (or maybe has dealt with, now) but there should have been something that caused that initial fallout with Luke. Would be interesting to see if the next films dig into that a bit. It definitely seems to be something more than a simple thirst for power - he seems to regret the choices he’s making, but somehow believes that taking the dark side is something that he has no choice about.

- I liked Rey’s character. She’s got this big thing where she’s totally mentally anchored to Jakku. There are a few things that she’s decided on - she lives on Jakku, she’s poor, she salvages stuff and she’s waiting for her parents. For most of the movie, she actively rejects any decision that could change one of those constants, and tries to get back to her old life as soon as possible, even when that doesn’t make sense. It’s like she’s deliberately gotten herself stuck in a rut because the thought of doing anything else is too painful. When she finally decides she needs to take action, though, it’s an awesome progression and I’m looking forward to seeing what happens next.

- That said, the whole ‘magical girl born into slavery’ thing is getting a bit tropey at this point, but that’s just the genre we’re in. It works well enough.

- I’m not shipping Rey and Finn, particularly after Finn got all grabby in the first part of the movie and Rey can obviously take care of herself. I can totally see Rey treating Finn like a little brother, though - there’s obviously some affection there. I’m hoping the obvious nods the film makes towards a relationship are a little _too_ obvious. The internet seems to think Poe/Finn is a much better ship, which I can get behind.

- Maz was a lot of fun. I think I got more upset about her cantina being destroyed than the entire republic homeworld system. It definitely had more buildup, anyway.

- The whole deal with the map and how the different bits of it fit together felt really cheap. I hope there’s a more interesting explanation (was Luke deliberately leaving breadcrumbs for the right person? Did he program R2 to activate at the right moment?) but if they drop it completely that might be for the best.

- Jakku is an obvious Tatooine analogue, except apparently they have cheaper water (enough to fill paddling pools with and feed to the animals) and Finn/Poe both manage to cross a large patch of desert without breaking into too much of a sweat. It’s definitely a milder desert. Tatooine-lite, maybe?

- Leia/Han’s ongoing romance was really well done, and provided the saddest bits of the movie.

- The movie doesn’t delve into the politics very much (probably for the better) but unless I missed something it leaves the relative sizes of the Republic/Empire as a mystery. Is this like the original trilogy, where the Empire is dominant, like the prequels where the Republic rule, or like the KOTOR continuity where they’re roughly equal? We may never (need to) know, I guess.

- It felt like we were supposed to recognise the old guy that Poe meets right at the beginning, but I had no idea who he was.

But yeah, it all felt very well made, definitely enjoyable, but not a totally amazing movie just yet. Assuming the trilogy carries on like this, though, I’m really looking forward to seeing the rest of it.

Edits:

- I’m really glad they didn’t stretch out the whole “Finn lied about being in the Resistance” thing too far for cheap drama. That sort of thing can be _really_ frustrating unless it’s handled well. Instead they played it for laughs when Han (being an old scammer) figured it out instantly, and implied that Rey didn’t take him too seriously anyway.

- Overall I think they were definitely playing it safe compared to the prequels, for obvious reasons. That probably wasn’t a bad decision, though
